The relief organisation

Trained volunteers, first on the ground after disaster.

The Humanitarian Relief Society is a registered NGO built for one job: getting trained people to floods, earthquakes, landslides and riots, quickly and without regard for who they're helping.

What it's built to do

HRS positions itself as a trained response force, not just a fundraising body.

Search & rescue

Volunteers complete a 21-day rescue and search-operation training programme before joining active response teams.

Disaster relief

Responding to floods, earthquakes and landslides with immediate aid, coordinated through state-level chapters.

Riot response

Providing emergency relief and rehabilitation support to victims of communal violence, alongside JIH's central relief coordination.

Community welfare

Running welfare drives — including anti-addiction campaigns and cleanliness initiatives — outside of emergency periods.

By the numbers, in Karnataka

During the 2020 COVID-19 lockdown, its Karnataka chapter alone reported distributing roughly ₹1.6 crore (about $200,000) worth of food kits to poor and stranded migrant workers, regardless of religion.

Where it sits

HRS is one of several welfare-facing bodies in the Jamaat network — a close cousin, rather than a duplicate, of the others.

HRS

Emergency-facing: rescue, disaster and riot relief, run by trained volunteer teams.

HRD Cell

A separate, internal JIH department focused on training and leadership development — easy to confuse by name, distinct in purpose.

In Kerala, an equivalent role is played by a differently-named body, Peoples Foundation, which coordinates a wider mix of welfare projects — from housing and scholarships to de-addiction and blood-donation drives.
