The youth wing, from Kerala

A youth movement that took to the street as much as the seminar room.

Solidarity Youth Movement grew out of a 2002 decision to split SIO's Kerala chapter into two: a student body, and a youth movement built for public campaigning. Two decades on, it's the most visibly activist part of the Jamaat family.

Founded
13 May 2003, Kozhikode
Headquarters
Hira Centre, Kozhikode, Kerala
Reach
Strongest in Kerala; chapters in other states

How it began

Solidarity exists because SIO, in Kerala, chose to specialise.

  1. 2002

    SIO's Kerala unit decides to narrow its own focus to students, leaving a gap for the youth organising it had also been doing.

  2. 2003

    Solidarity Youth Movement is launched on 13 May at Muthalakulam Grounds, Calicut, with Dr Koottil Mohammed Ali elected its first state president.

  3. 2005

    The movement holds its first state conference in Palakkad, formalising a structure that would grow to cover the whole state.

  4. 2016

    Solidarity organises an International Islamophobia Conference in Kozhikode, one of several public events built around that theme.

  5. Since

    The model has been carried to other states — a Solidarity Karnataka chapter now runs alongside the original Kerala movement.

What it organises around

Solidarity's own materials describe its cause in the language of liberation and resistance, tied to a longer history of Muslim mobilisation in Kerala.

Anti-Islamophobia campaigning

Public conferences, caravans and legislative demands aimed at what it calls rising Islamophobia and Hindutva majoritarianism.

Legal activism

Court challenges against laws it views as discriminatory, including the National Investigation Agency Act.

Ecological & social justice

Campaigns on sustainable development, unlawful detention and profiling, framed as part of a wider justice agenda.

Note: On some of these issues, Solidarity has found common cause with India's left-wing parties, particularly around opposition to globalisation and to laws it sees as targeting Muslims — an alignment its own literature is explicit about, even though its worldview remains rooted in Islamic teaching rather than in secular left politics.
